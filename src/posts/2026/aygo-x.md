I grow up in a family with a car in HK. Nothing fancy, but still a privileged, I know. Some second hand Mitsubishi Pajero iO, and then second hand Toyota Corolla, and then second hand Honda Civic or Accord. All from the 90s or early 2000s, no fancy stereos, no exotic interiors, and several with leaky/stuck sunroof. They (along with a lot of things in my life) slowly but surely nudge me to getting a car, even in yet another world-renowned-good-public-transport-city.

## ÖPNV is good but not great.

Berlin is consider having one of the best public transport systems in the world. Of course my home town HK is always going to be streets ahead, but Berlin is definitely no slouch. It's easy to use, it has wide variety, D-Ticket is a pretty good deal, and combine with the fact that Berlin is not super huge and is mostly flat, you can get almost anywhere in 45 minutes, unlike HK where every trip take an hour and more. 

However, it also has become a running joke that BVG & Deutsche Bahn are not doing very well in recent years, especially when it comes to reliability. Bus are always late, and in recent years many lines have their frequency reduced; U-Bahn haven't changed too much, but it's U-Bahn, from the chaos of U8 to the frequent closure of various lines for maintenance / expansion, means you should not just trust your memory of which line goes where; and last but not least we have DB, the butt of the joke at this point, on how unreliable and broken and expensive it has been, and will be for years to come if/when the infrastructure catches up.

And for my daily life, a small city car is an improvement over public transport. I mostly stick to home-gym-work, which I either have access to private parking, or the place has sufficient public parking for cheap. I still have my D-Ticket, which allows me to just hop on to a Tram (the best ÖPNV at the moment) if my colleague and I decided to go for a spontaneous bar after work, but also as someone who can afford it, I feel like I am doing my (tiny) part to keep BVG funded to keep the lights on.

## E-scooter can only go so far

A few years back, deep in pandemic, I bough an e-scooter (Niu KQi1 Pro) so that I can travel a bit more freely, avoid germs, and commute on my own schedule, especially since BVG changed it from every 10 minutes to 20 minutes. I don't know if it would ended up being a good idea so I bought a relatively cheap one, which I'm not regretting per se, but it's definitely a "what you pay is what you get" situation. The low range makes it virtually impossible to do much besides work commute, and the lack of suspension makes it pretty uncomfortable for all the cobblestones in Berlin. Combine with the speed limit of 20km/h (which is fair given how sketchy e-scooters can be, but still), it doesn't take long before it becomes a non-option. 

## Having a car is too cheap in Berlin & Germany

Automobile is a key pillar of Germany, so no sane politician is going to do anything to make owning and using a car expensive. And as someone from HK, which does everything they can to discourage people from having private cars, it is still a bit of a shock of how cheap owning & driving a car can be. Petrol is (TODO percentage) cheaper than HK; Somehow Sunday parking is free virtually everywhere; and most stupidly resident parking is like 20€ for 2 years! Yeah you get bird poop and maybe risk of people stealing your expensive tires from your equally expensive Mercades-Benz, but still it is so so so much cheaper to have a car over here. Back in the days in HK, my family was paying around 50€ a month to (hypothetical) triad just to get a spot in the open field to park, and the car is always cooked under the sun and the paint job start fading in no time!

And if you have followed all the public transport YouTubers, you probably know the following already: Cars are loud, doesn't matter if it's ICE or BEV or anything in between; they are expensive to the city cause maintaining roads for multi-ton object rolling on it all the time is a difficult job; pollution from tail pipe kills both people and the planet; and accidents happens, from reckless drivers to just someone who just happened to have a medical emergency while driving, and ended up killing pedestrians and cyclists. And yet somehow Germany is charging pretty damn small amount of money for what car does. Like with the recent spike of oil prices from the war in Iran, IMO the tax deduction is not necessary after all, both because it wasn't really effectively in helping drivers to reduce expenses, while also continue to effectively subsidize driving.

For states and places where driving is necessary, sure they need to keep driving a somewhat affordable thing, that's fine. But with the dozens and dozens of cities with good to great public transport, Germany should jack up the price of having and driving a car. Charge a lot more for parking; charge more for fuel; use the money we can afford to pay (cause we can afford to get a car, which is a lot more than what a lot of people can ask for) to make public transport better, or even feed to the government to help lift up other cities and towns make public transport the default option in those places as well. 

## (For now) It helps with my mental health

## It unlocks a lot of travel opportunities

A few years ago, an old colleague of mine show me a DB BahnCard 50 discount, which I used a quite a few times. And after like the 4th big city I went to, I realized it's kinda boring. They all have like a Hauptbahnhof that is sketchy; they all have a big church/cathedral in various degree of epic-ness, and they all have mediocre food. Not that DB don't have stops that are not big cities, but often have not so frequent services which significantly limit my travel options; or their tickets are stupidly expensive. Having my own transport means I can just follow my own schedule, or even throw it out the window and just wing it. Like 2 months ago, on a Friday afternoon, I saw that finally we have the first sunny weekend after a long winter, so I just start Googling beaches I can visit, booked one night stay at a hotel in Poland along the Baltic Sea, and just drive there when I wake up at like 7am the next day. And when I need to drive back, I just drive after I am done exploring the area, no need to try to rush or wait to follow the time table, as long as I know how many hours I have to drive. I have done several more spontaneous trips since then, and so far I enjoyed most of them.

## This car is green(-ish)

I'm not talking about the color, I'm talking about how it affects the environment. Yes a BEV would be even better, but as someone with no access to home charging anytime soon, and for someone who wants to do more road trips and visit weirder and weirder places, a BEV is way more expensive than I can ever justify it to be. Toyota Aygo X (Hybrid) is marketed as the lowest emission car on sale that doesn't have a plug (i.e. not BEV or PHEV), thanks to its hybrid power train and extremely compact size & weight. And with the right condition, it could be one of the greener transport methods for vacation, except trains, but we all know DB is currently a shit show, and the last thing I want to experience during my vacations is shit show. 

And it would all be a moo point if this car stop working shortly, which is why I choose Toyota first. Its reputation of being the most reliable car brand on the planet, could in theory means I can drive this car for maybe 15 years or more, sipping tiny amount of fuel along the way, while I wait for EV tech and battery tech to catch up to what I want it to be.
