And that leads to the next point and the backstory:</p><p><strong>Your most expensive camera might not be your best choice</strong></p><p>It was 2022 I believe, I have (to this day) a <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujifilm_X-T2" rel="noreferrer">Fujifilm X-T2</a> from 2016, and with a few descent prime lenses. So when I first get started, it seems logical to use the best camera I have. However, once I saw the clips with all the flickering, I know I have to re-think everything.</p><ul><li>It does very minimal processing = it has absolutely terrible PWM flickering. </li><li>It has really short internal recording limit (10 min). After all it was already 6+ years old.</li><li>If it is in <a href="https://fujifilm-x.com/en-gb/learning-centre/quick-start-guide-fujifilm-x-t2/" rel="noreferrer">"Boost Mode"</a> for too long, it overheats and shut itself off......</li></ul><p>On the other hand, while my iPhone 11 Pro is not the latest model, and has way smaller sensor size, it is however designed to allow beginner like me to shoot, and get useable footage 95% of the time. So that's what I ended up using for most of the cases to this day.</p><p><strong>Lightning &amp; Audio are very important</strong></p><p>While it is more popular / common to discuss what camera or lens to use, given that I am recording basically just (unfortunately statistically speaking) 1 guy the corner of the room, the image quality &amp; cinematography isn't really what people pay attention to. <strong>It is the content that people are after</strong>, and if the video doesn't have something too distracting, that would be nice.</p><p>For useable content, apart from making sure the exposure to the projector screen/TV is correct, the most important is definitely the audio. I have always been a gadget nerd, so I already know a little bit about different kinds of microphone exist. So it doesn't take long for me to figure out lavalier microphone ("lav" for short) is what I need. And another hour of YouTube video watching later, I found a specific model that is not too expensive &amp; has good quality, and that's what I have been using to this date.</p><p>Now the content is audible, the last major hurdle I ran into was noise, visual camera sensor noise. Sometimes the rooms are too dark, and it makes the speaker and their movement distracting.....</p><figure class="kg-card kg-embed-card kg-card-hascaption"><iframe width="200" height="113" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/krACelNXh7I?start=921&amp;feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen="" title="OpinionGPT: Exploring the Impact of Biases on Large Language Models - Ansar Aynetdinov"></iframe><figcaption><p><span style="white-space: pre-wrap;">(If the timestamp doesn't work, go to 15:21)</span></p></figcaption></figure><p>This was recorded (from X-T2 via HDMI to OBS) during <a href="https://devfest.berlin" rel="noreferrer">DevFest Berlin 2023</a>, and the image quality drop so much when the light, graciously load by the venue for free, ran out of juice. Thankfully this was more of a lecture-hall setup, so I don't have to worry too much about speaking walking around and pointing things "in the dark" for the camera, but still.</p><p>Now before events, apart from setting the camera up, I will also try to see if it is possible to have some spotlight pointing at/near the speaker, and in a lot of office space &amp; event space, all you have to do is ask 👍</p><p><strong>Learn how to edit</strong></p><p>Just like photography, the videos you get from the camera is not going to be perfect. But unlike photography, there is actually some (semi-)professional-grade software that you can use for free! If you have a macOS device, you can get started on <a href="https://www.apple.com/in/imovie/" rel="noreferrer">iMovie</a>. I made this back in 2015, and I'd say apart from the resolution, it held up not as bad as I thought!</p><figure class="kg-card kg-embed-card"><iframe width="200" height="113" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/uuANpqr89ZU?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen="" title="Cappdocia Hot Air Ballon Ride"></iframe></figure><p>But if you want to get serious, I would highly recommend <a href="https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve" rel="noreferrer">DaVinci Resolve</a>. It is a Freemium software, that has most of the bells and whistles that one might need. Cropping, stitching, colour correction, and most recently, trying to reduce the flickering of those god damn PWM LEDs again. </p><p>But most importantly, because it is a popular software, there are countless resources online. If I want to do something, I just go to YouTube and find the latest tutorial. From what is &amp; how to do J/L-cut, how to fade-in/out &amp; other animations, how to (try) to rescue some audio clip, and more recently, what does the Fusion tab actually do.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-embed-card kg-card-hascaption"><iframe width="200" height="113" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/BGLEnfrV7LI?start=1272&amp;feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen="" title="Large Language Models: A New Era of Augmented Analytics - Paul Egorov"></iframe><figcaption><p><span style="white-space: pre-wrap;">I learned how to animate audio transition &amp; (attempt) to apply noise reduction around 21:12, when the I boosted the lav mic to get some of the room speaker audio to an audible-ish level.</span></p></figcaption></figure><p><strong>Lastly, Double check everything</strong></p><ul><li>Forgot to charge my phone or the microphones (or both)</li><li>Forgot to turn on the microphone &amp; got a whole session of no audio</li><li>Forgot to bring the right cable to connect the wireless mic receiver to my phone, because for some dumb reason the 3.5mm requires different pinout for camera audio and phone audio 🤬</li><li>Forgot to make sure there is enough storage on the phone, cause I was just travelling and downloaded too much music &amp; movies to watch on the plane ✈️</li><li>Forgot to unplug the charging cable, and the phone stops recording cause it overheats.....</li></ul><hr><p>Hope this helps if you want to get started on low(-ish) budget Meetup recording. And if you have any tips &amp; tricks, or questions to ask, just leave a comment below, or DM me on any of the social media platform that is not run by (allegedly) bigots 🤷</p>
